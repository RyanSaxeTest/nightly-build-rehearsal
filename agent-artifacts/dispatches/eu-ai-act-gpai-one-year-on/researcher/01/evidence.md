# Evidence: dispatches/eu-ai-act-gpai-one-year-on (researcher 01)

The Regulation text on EUR-Lex owns every obligation the commission asks
about: the four duties on all providers in Article 53, the 10^25 FLOP
presumption in Article 51, the two-week notification in Article 52, the
four extra duties on systemic-risk providers in Article 55, the fine ceiling
in Article 101, the 2 August 2027 deadline for models already on the market in
Article 111(3), and the application dates in Article 113. The Commission's own
pages establish the 10^23 FLOP indicative criterion, the one-third compute rule
for modifiers, the open-source conditions, the first-year good-faith stance,
what adherence to the Code of Practice does and does not give a provider, and
the Commission's list of 21 signatories plus xAI on one chapter, current to
31 July 2026. The enforcement record is thin. The Commission's 31 July 2026
announcement says enforcement begins on 2 August 2026 and names no provider.
The only action on the record is a set of requests for information to more
than 30 providers, which reaches this record through one paywalled Agence
Europe paragraph and no Commission page. No source opened names any model
designated or notified as systemic-risk, and the Article 52(6) list was not
found. Meta's refusal to sign rests on CNBC's account of a LinkedIn post, not
the post itself. This record was capped at eight sources on the orchestrator's
instruction; the guidelines PDF, the Code chapters, and the Digital Omnibus
text were not opened.

Kind:        primary. Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the Artificial Intelligence
             Act, as published on EUR-Lex. It owns every obligation, threshold,
             fine, and date below.
Establishes: Article 3(63): a "general-purpose AI model" is an AI model,
             including one "trained with a large amount of data using
             self-supervision at scale, that displays significant generality
             and is capable of competently performing a wide range of
             distinct tasks regardless of the way the model is placed on the
             market and that can be integrated into a variety of downstream
             systems or applications", excluding models used for research,
             development or prototyping before placement on the market.
             Article 3(64) and (65) define "high-impact capabilities" as
             those that "match or exceed the capabilities recorded in the
             most advanced general-purpose AI models" and "systemic risk" as
             a risk specific to those capabilities with "a significant impact
             on the Union market".
             Article 51(1): a model is classified as systemic-risk if it has
             high impact capabilities "evaluated on the basis of appropriate
             technical tools and methodologies, including indicators and
             benchmarks", or by Commission decision under Annex XIII criteria.
             Article 51(2): the presumption applies "when the cumulative amount
             of computation used for its training measured in floating point
             operations is greater than 10^25". Article 51(3): the Commission
             may amend the thresholds by delegated act.
             Article 52(1): a provider whose model meets the condition "shall
             notify the Commission without delay and in any event within two
             weeks after that requirement is met or it becomes known that it
             will be met". Article 52(2): the provider may argue the model
             does not present systemic risks despite meeting the threshold.
             Article 52(4): the Commission may designate a model ex officio
             or after a qualified alert from the scientific panel. Article
             52(6): "The Commission shall ensure that a list of
             general-purpose AI models with systemic risk is published and
             shall keep that list up to date".
             Article 53(1): every provider shall (a) draw up and keep up to
             date technical documentation, containing at least Annex XI, for
             the AI Office and national authorities on request; (b) make
             information and documentation available to downstream AI-system
             providers, containing at least Annex XII; (c) "put in place a
             policy to comply with Union law on copyright and related rights",
             including identifying rights reservations under Article 4(3) of
             Directive (EU) 2019/790; (d) "draw up and make publicly available
             a sufficiently detailed summary about the content used for
             training of the general-purpose AI model, according to a
             template provided by the AI Office".
             Article 53(2): duties (a) and (b) do not apply to models released
             under a free and open-source licence with parameters, weights,
             architecture and usage information public; "This exception shall
             not apply to general-purpose AI models with systemic risks."
             Article 53(4): providers may rely on codes of practice under
             Article 56 to demonstrate compliance until a harmonised standard
             is published; harmonised standards grant a presumption of
             conformity; providers who neither adhere to an approved code nor
             comply with a standard "shall demonstrate alternative adequate
             means of compliance for assessment by the Commission".
             Article 55(1): systemic-risk providers shall in addition (a)
             perform model evaluation "in accordance with standardised
             protocols and tools reflecting the state of the art, including
             conducting and documenting adversarial testing"; (b) assess and
             mitigate possible systemic risks at Union level; (c) "keep track
             of, document, and report, without undue delay, to the AI Office"
             relevant information about serious incidents; (d) "ensure an
             adequate level of cybersecurity protection" for the model and
             its physical infrastructure. Article 55(2) mirrors 53(4) for
             codes, standards, and alternative means.
             Article 101(1): the Commission may fine providers "not exceeding
             3 % of their annual total worldwide turnover in the preceding
             financial year or EUR 15 000 000, whichever is higher" where the
             provider intentionally or negligently (a) infringed the relevant
             provisions, (b) failed to comply with an Article 91 information
             request "or supplied incorrect, incomplete or misleading
             information", (c) failed to comply with an Article 93 measure,
             or (d) refused model access for an Article 92 evaluation.
             Article 111(3): "Providers of general-purpose AI models that have
             been placed on the market before 2 August 2025 shall take the
             necessary steps in order to comply with the obligations laid
             down in this Regulation by 2 August 2027."
             Article 113: the Regulation "shall apply from 2 August 2026",
             except that under point (b) Chapter V (general-purpose AI models)
             applies "from 2 August 2025, with the exception of Article 101".
             Article 101's 2 August 2026 date therefore follows from the
             general date, not from a line naming it. Signed at Brussels,
             13 June 2024.
Paraphrase:  Four duties bind every provider from 2 August 2025; four more
             bind providers of models presumed systemic-risk at 10^25 FLOP;
             fines up to 3 percent or EUR 15 million apply from 2 August 2026;
             models already on the market before 2 August 2025 have until
             2 August 2027.
Locators:    Article 3 points (63) to (65); Chapter V, Articles 51, 52, 53,
             55; Chapter XII, Article 101; Chapter XIII, Articles 111 and 113.
             The EUR-Lex HTML view drops the superscript, so Article 51(2)
             renders as "greater than 1025"; the figure is 10^25.
Quote:       Article 53(4): "Providers of general-purpose AI models who do not
             adhere to an approved code of practice or do not comply with a
             European harmonised standard shall demonstrate alternative
             adequate means of compliance for assessment by the Commission."

### Contradictions

- Who is in the systemic-risk tier. The commission asks who has been
  designated or presumed in. Article 52(6) obliges the Commission to publish a
  list. No page opened carries such a list, no source names a notified or
  designated model, and a search for the list found only the article text and
  commentary. Not found is not the same as not published; the gap stands
  until someone finds the list or a Commission statement that none exists.
- Date of the information requests. Agence Europe says the Commission
  "specified on Tuesday 1 September" and that Virkkunen "wrote on Sunday",
  which is 30 August 2026. Unopened search-result pages date her confirmation
  to 29 August. Use Agence Europe; the aggregators were not read.
- Recipients of the information requests. Search-result summaries name
  OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google, attributed to a Euractiv exclusive. Neither
  Euractiv nor any page naming recipients was opened. The visible Agence
  Europe text names no provider. Do not name recipients from this record.
- Virkkunen's title. Agence Europe writes "Commission Vice-President". Help
  Net Security (discarded below) writes "Executive Vice-President for Tech
  Sovereignty, Security and Democracy". Neither is the Commission's own
  page. Take the title from a Commission page before it reaches a headline.
- Kaplan's title. CNBC writes "global affairs chief". Meta's own styling was
  not opened. A headline should not carry a title this record cannot source
  to Meta.
- What signing the Code buys. The Code page promises "more legal certainty
  and trust than if they proved compliance through other methods". The
  Commission's Q&A and Article 53(4) both say adherence gives no presumption
  of conformity; only harmonised standards do. Both statements can be true.
  The writer should not upgrade "legal certainty" into a safe harbour.
- Teeth. The guidelines Q&A describes the year to 2 August 2026 as good-faith
  collaboration and says full enforcement "including through fines" starts
  then. The action on the record since is information requests. No fine, no
  ordered measure, and no model evaluation appears in any source opened. The
  commission's framing, that the powers now exist and the first use is on
  the record, holds; a claim that any provider has been sanctioned does not.
- The Digital Omnibus. Search results from law-firm summaries say the
  Omnibus agreed on 7 May 2026 left the general-purpose model obligations
  unchanged. The Council's press release returned 403 to both fetch tools
  and none of the summaries was opened. This record cannot support any
  sentence about the Omnibus.
- Retraining under the 2027 deadline. Article 111(3) sets the date. Whether a
  provider must retrain a pre-August-2025 model to satisfy the copyright
  policy or training summary is answered, if anywhere, in the guidelines PDF,
  which was not opened. The two Commission Q&A pages read say nothing on it.
- The 180 signatories. The 31 July 2026 announcement's "more than 180
  organisations" signed the Code of Practice on transparency of AI-generated
  content, a separate instrument. The general-purpose code's list is 21 plus
  xAI. Conflating the two would inflate the general-purpose count eightfold.
